# Style Learning Rules

Use when analyzing inspiration slideshows from another account or niche.

## What To Extract

Extract transferable design intelligence:
- slide pacing and funnel structure
- hook format and emotional rhythm
- text hierarchy and emphasis system
- font category, weight, italic/outline/shadow treatment
- palette logic: background colors, text colors, accents, contrast, warmth/coolness, repeated color relationships
- background simplicity/detail level
- natural photo vs animated/graphic treatment
- product reveal timing and product placement style
- composition patterns and negative-space strategy

## What To Ignore

Do not transfer:
- the inspiration product category if the user's product is different
- skincare/cosmetics claims into unrelated products
- exact product use cases
- exact scenes/backgrounds
- exact creator palette colors
- fake social proof, reviews, awards, or unsupported claims

## Palette Rule

If an inspiration creator has a recognizable palette, analyze the palette logic but build a different palette for the user.

Capture things like:
- muted vs saturated
- pastel vs bold
- warm vs cool
- high contrast vs soft contrast
- background/text/accent relationship
- whether background colors are simple flat fields or natural scene colors

Then propose an original palette using the same design logic, not copied colors.

## Background Simplicity Rule

Some high-performing slideshows use very simple backgrounds. Do not automatically make these more complex.

- If the target is animated/graphic/CGC-style, simple palette backgrounds may be correct.
- If the target is normal realistic product photography, make backgrounds more natural and believable while keeping good negative space for text.

## Continuity Rule

Repeated environments should feel coherent across the slideshow. Keep the same redesigned location/style world through consistent light direction, color family, material palette, and repeated natural props. Vary camera angle, crop, action, and product position so slides do not look duplicated.
